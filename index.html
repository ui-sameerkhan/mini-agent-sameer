Business Model

The coordination layer between patient and hospital

A medical tourism facilitator sits between the patient and the Indian healthcare system. Core services span the entire journey:

🌐

Lead Gen & Pre-Consultation

Website, local marketing/agent network and telemedicine screening in the source country.

🩺

Clinical Matching

Connecting the patient's case — reports, scans, diagnosis — to the right specialist and hospital, with a written cost estimate.

🛂

Visa Support

Preparing the hospital invitation letter and documentation for the e-Medical / e-Medical Attendant visa.

✈️

Travel & Logistics

Flights, airport pickup, accommodation for patient and attendants, local transport.

🤝

On-Ground Concierge

Interpreter, hospital liaison, discharge coordination, sightseeing/wellness add-ons during recovery.

📋

Post-Treatment

Teleconsultation follow-up, physiotherapy coordination, records handover to the home-country doctor.

Two positioning models — most successful facilitators blend both

Hospital-Commission Model

Hospital pays, patient doesn't

Hospital pays you 10–25% of the treatment package as a referral/marketing fee; patient pays no extra fee.

Best for: high-volume, price-sensitive corridors (Africa, Bangladesh)

Concierge / Fee-for-Service

Premium, transparent experience

Patient pays a flat USD 500–2,500 service fee directly for full concierge support; you may still receive a smaller hospital fee.

Best for: higher-value GCC / CIS patients

Legal Structure, Registration & Licensing

No single licence — a compliance checklist

There is no single "medical tourism licence" in India — the business runs on standard company registration plus healthcare-specific accreditation, built up roughly in this order.

Core registration — do first

  • 1Register a Private Limited Company with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs — credibility with hospitals and lets you raise investment later
  • 2Apply for PAN and TAN for the company
  • 3Register for GST — commission income is a taxable service; export-of-services benefits apply (zero-rated GST with a Letter of Undertaking)
  • 4Register under the state Shops & Establishments Act for your office
  • 5Open a current account with an AD bank for FEMA-compliant inward foreign remittances
  • 6Udyam/MSME registration — optional, unlocks collateral-free loans and tender eligibility

Sector accreditation — within Year 1

  • 1NABH "Medical Value Travel Facilitator" (MVTF) accreditation — increasingly expected by top hospital chains before empanelment
  • 2Ministry of Tourism recognition under the National Medical & Wellness Tourism Promotion Board
  • 3FSSAI food licence — if arranging meals/catering for patients and attendants
  • 4Professional indemnity / liability insurance
  • 5Empanelment MOUs with 3–5 NABH/JCI-accredited hospitals — commission %, exclusivity and payment cycle in writing
  • 6DPDP Act 2023 compliance — written data-protection and consent policy for medical records and passport data
  • 7Trademark registration for your brand name

Marketing & Patient Acquisition

Trust beats ad spend in this category

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Local Presence

A bilingual representative or agent in each priority country builds trust faster than digital ads alone — many patients decide via word-of-mouth and diaspora networks.

💻

Digital

Google Ads and Meta/WhatsApp campaigns targeting procedure + "India treatment cost" locally; a content-rich site with itemised pricing converts better than vague "contact us" competitors.

🔗

Referral Network

General physicians, diagnostic labs and community/religious organisations are strong lead sources for planned surgeries like joint replacement.

🏛️

Embassy Relationships

Hospital chains are formally engaging embassies (e.g. Uzbekistan) to build structured referral pathways — we can position as that structured channel.

🎥

Testimonials

Patient testimonials and before/after mobility videos are unusually persuasive for orthopaedic procedures specifically.
